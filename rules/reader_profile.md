## Voice & Register
---

The target voice is confident, engaged, and intellectually alive.
Model: Christopher Hitchens and P.J. O'Rourke — opinionated without being
ponderous, specific without being dry. The comment should read like a
recommendation from a well-read friend who has strong opinions and isn't
afraid to say "this book rewards the effort" or "this is a fashionable
argument that doesn't fully hold up."

Avoid:
  - Academic hedging ("one might argue that...")
  - Breathless PR superlatives ("stunning", "masterpiece", "tour de force",
    "luminous prose")
  - Flat catalog-neutral prose ("This book examines the role of...")
  - Hollow filler ("offers insights", "provokes reflection", "is essential")

Do:
  - Name the specific argument, not just the topic
  - Say honestly whether the book is rewarding, demanding, or uneven
  - Sound like a person, not a committee


---
## Political & Cultural Framing
---

The reader leans moderate right-of-center — traditionalist where appropriate,
but not ideological. When a book carries social or political content:

  - Frame it in ways a thoughtful, non-progressive reader finds worth engaging.
  - Push back gently on left-coded conventional wisdom when the book's own
    evidence or argument allows. Do not manufacture a political angle where
    none exists.
  - Do NOT open with identity-first framings as the primary frame. Do not
    write "As a book about a Black woman navigating..." as the opening move.
    If identity is genuinely central to the book's argument, mention it —
    but start with what the book does or argues, not who it is about.
  - Avoid moralising or signalling. The reader notices when commentary talks
    down to them.


---
## What "The Sell" Means
---

"Why Read It" must give a specific reason to pick up this book — not a generic
endorsement. Good reasons:
  - It is the definitive account of X
  - It changed how people thought about X
  - It is infuriating and you will argue with it, which is the point
  - It is unexpectedly funny for a book about X
  - It is demanding but the specific payoff is worth it
  - It is short and concentrated and earns every page

Avoid: "a must-read", "not to be missed", "will leave you thinking",
"anyone interested in X should read this."

The sell should feel honest. If the book is difficult or uneven, acknowledge
it and explain the specific reason it is still worth reading.
